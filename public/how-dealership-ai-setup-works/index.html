Four steps, then it's yours

Setting up AI at a dealership doesn't have to be a six-month software rollout. Our process is four simple steps, built around your people and the tools you already have. You pay once for the setup, and when we're done, your team runs it.

Step 1: Book a call

Tell us your role and what's eating your day. It might be the report you rebuild every morning, the reviews you never have time to check, or follow-ups that slip through the cracks. We'll ask how your store works today, which reports and inboxes you rely on, and what tools you already have access to.

This first conversation is also where we're honest about fit. If an AI agent isn't the right answer for a problem, we'll say so.

Step 2: We map it out

Next, we look at the actual work: the reports, the inbox, the follow-ups, and the repetitive tasks. Then we pick what AI should handle first.

What we look for

  • Work that repeats. Daily or weekly tasks that follow the same pattern
  • Work built from information you already have. Reports, CSV exports, emails, and review sites
  • Work where a draft or alert is enough. The agent prepares, and a person decides
  • Low risk, real time saved. We start where a mistake is cheap and the time saved is obvious

What we usually leave for later

  • Anything that sends messages to customers without a person checking it first
  • Anything that changes records in your DMS or CRM
  • Sensitive financing or credit data, unless it fits your written security program

The result is a short plan: which agents to build first, what each one reads, what it produces, and who receives it.

Step 3: We build it with you

This is where the dealer experience and the prompt expertise come together. We write the prompts and set up the agents and workflows in your own AI tools, side by side with the people who'll use them.

What the setup includes

  • Clear instructions. Each agent gets a prompt that covers the job, the sources, what counts as important, the format, and the limits. You're only as good as your prompt, so this is where we spend the most care. See examples in our Grok Bot prompts for car dealerships.
  • The right connections. Only the sources each job needs, using official sign-ins. Read-only access comes first.
  • A schedule. For example, a morning brief before the sales meeting, or a review check twice a day.
  • Approval rules. Anything customer-facing, record-changing, or price-related waits for a person's OK. Tools like Grok Bot support this kind of "ask first" rule natively (Grok Bot docs).
  • Testing on real days. We run the workflows against your actual reports and inboxes and tighten the instructions until the output is something your team trusts.

Step 4: You run it

We show you how everything works so your team can use it and adjust it on their own. That includes how to change a prompt when a report changes, how to add a new alert, and how to pause something that isn't pulling its weight.

There's no monthly fee and no contract keeping you tied to us. The agents live in your accounts. If you want help with more roles later, you can bring us back. You never have to.

What it doesn't involve

  • No new software to buy from us. We work in AI tools you already have access to.
  • No rip-and-replace. Your DMS and CRM stay right where they are.
  • No subscriptions or long-term contracts. One fee for the setup.

Where most stores start

The most common first projects are the ones on our homepage: a GM's morning brief built from existing reports, CSVs, and emails; review alerts for Google, Yelp, and other sites; complaint monitoring in a shared inbox; and on-demand answers about pace, people, and performance. From there, stores usually add the next role, whether that's sales managers, service, F&I, or the BDC. See who we help.

FAQ

How do I set up AI agents at my dealership?

Start with one repetitive job for one person, connect only the information that job needs, write clear instructions with limits, add approval rules for anything customer-facing, and review the results for a few weeks before expanding.

Do we need to change our DMS or CRM?

No. Nothing gets ripped out. The agents usually work from the reports and exports your systems already produce.

Is there an ongoing fee?

No. Lidonick charges a one-time fee for the setup. There's no subscription and no long-term contract.

Will the AI contact customers on its own?

Not unless you decide it should. We set approval rules so customer messages, record changes, and pricing wait for a person's OK.

What happens after setup?

Your team runs it. We show them how to adjust prompts, add alerts, and pause workflows, so they aren't dependent on us.
