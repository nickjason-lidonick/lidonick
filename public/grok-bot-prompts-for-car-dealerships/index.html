Grok Bot Prompts for Car Dealerships: Copy-Ready Examples for Every Role

An AI agent can only do what you clearly tell it to do. Most disappointing setups don't fail because the tool is weak. They fail because the instructions were one vague sentence.

Below is the formula we use for dealer prompts, followed by examples for each department. Copy them, swap in your store's details, and adjust from there. They're written for Grok Bot, but they work with most AI agents.

The five parts of a good dealer prompt

  1. Job: what to do, for whom, and how often. ("Every weekday at 7:30 AM, for the GM…")
  2. Sources: exactly where to look. ("…the DMS sales report emailed at 6 AM and the service RO export in the shared Drive folder…")
  3. Bar: what matters and what to ignore. ("…flag any deal pending more than 3 days…")
  4. Format: what the result looks like. ("…five bullets max, numbers first, sent to me in chat…")
  5. Limits: what it must never do. ("…never email a customer, never change a record, and if a number is missing, say so instead of guessing.")

Leave out any of the five and the agent has to guess. Guessing is where bad results come from.

GM and dealer principal prompts

Morning brief

Every weekday at 7:30 AM, read the daily sales report and the service summary that arrive in my inbox overnight, plus the OEM objective sheet in our shared Drive folder. Send me one brief with: units delivered month-to-date and pending, pace against the OEM objective given the selling days left, each salesperson's units and open deals, and yesterday's service RO count and any open customer complaints. Keep it under 10 lines, numbers first. Use only figures from those files. If a file is missing or a number doesn't add up, say so at the top. Never reply to anyone or change any file.

On-demand question

Using only the latest inventory export in the Drive folder "Inventory," list every used unit over 60 days in stock, sorted by days in stock, with year, make, model, stock number, and list price. If the export is more than a day old, tell me before answering.

Sales manager and salesperson prompts

Daily follow-up list

Each morning, read the open-prospects export I save to the "Sales" folder. Build my call list for today: anyone I haven't contacted in 3+ days, anyone whose test drive was in the last 7 days with no deal, and any be-backs. For each one, give the name, the vehicle of interest, the last touch, and one sentence I could open with. Don't contact anyone. This is a list for me only.

Follow-up draft

Draft a short, friendly follow-up text to [customer] about the [vehicle] they drove on [day]. Mention one specific thing from my notes. No pricing, no pressure, under 300 characters. Show me the draft and wait. Never send it.

Service director and advisor prompts

Review alerts

Twice a day, check our Google and Yelp listings for new reviews that mention service. Alert me right away about any review of 3 stars or less, with the reviewer's name, the stars, the full text, and a suggested reply in a calm, owner-to-customer tone. Group 4- and 5-star reviews into one daily recap. Never post a reply. Drafts only, for my approval.

Tomorrow's drive

Every afternoon at 4 PM, read tomorrow's appointment export from the service folder. List the waiters, the loaner requests, and any job that mentions a part on order. Keep it to one screen. If the export is missing, tell me instead of guessing.

F&I prompts

Deal-jacket follow-up

Each morning, read the funding report I save to the "F&I" folder. List every deal not yet funded after 3 days and what's holding it up, based on the notes column. Don't include customer Social Security numbers, dates of birth, or credit details in your reply. Use deal numbers and last names only.

Keep F&I agents to the minimum data the job needs, and check the setup against your written security program under the FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314).

BDC and internet sales prompts

First-response drafts

When a new internet lead email arrives in the BDC inbox, draft a first reply within the hour that answers the customer's actual question, confirms the vehicle using our website listing, and offers two appointment times. Never quote a price or payment that isn't on the listing. Put every draft in a list for a BDC rep to approve. Don't send anything.

Missed-lead sweep

At 6 PM every day, compare today's new leads with the replies sent from the BDC inbox. List any lead that hasn't received a reply yet, oldest first.

Prompt mistakes we see most at dealerships

  • No limits. Leaving out "never send" or "never change" invites surprises. Write the limits every time, even when the tool has approval settings.
  • Vague sources. "Check my email" is too broad. Name the sender, the subject, or the folder.
  • No "I don't know" rule. Tell the agent to say when data is missing instead of filling the gap.
  • Too many jobs in one prompt. Give one Bot one clear job. Add another Bot for the next job.
  • Set it and forget it. Reports change, and so do people. Review and tighten your instructions every few weeks.

Frequently asked questions

Do these prompts only work in Grok Bot?

No. They're written with Grok Bot's routines and approvals in mind, but the five-part structure works with most AI assistants and agents.

How long should a dealership AI prompt be?

Long enough to cover the job, sources, bar, format, and limits. That's usually a short paragraph. Shorter prompts usually leave the agent guessing.

How do I stop an agent from messaging customers?

Put "never send" in the prompt, and also set an "Ask first" rule in Grok Bot's settings so any outgoing message waits for your approval.

Can I schedule a prompt to run every morning?

Yes. Grok Bot can turn a repeated task into a routine that runs on the schedule you set, like a 7:30 AM brief.

What data should I keep out of a prompt?

Passwords, Social Security numbers, credit details, and anything the job doesn't need. Use the tool's secure sign-in instead of pasting passwords.
