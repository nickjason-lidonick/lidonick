The problem

Dealers Are Paying for Software They Could Build Themselves

Ask any GM or dealer principal what the store pays for software every month and you'll usually get a sigh before the number. Every year brings another subscription, another long-term contract, and another tool that only does part of the job. Most of it is pitched as the thing that will finally save your people time.

Meanwhile, the AI assistants available today can already handle a lot of that work, as long as they're set up with the right instructions. The problem isn't the technology. Most stores just don't have the time or the know-how to set it up.

How the software stack keeps growing

No dealership decides to run on a pile of disconnected tools. It happens one problem at a time. Reviews are slipping, so someone signs up for a reputation tool. Leads are getting missed, so a lead-response add-on gets bolted onto the CRM. The GM wants a daily snapshot, so a reporting dashboard gets added. Each purchase made sense when it was made.

The result is a store that juggles many systems. In a CDK Global and NADA Academy survey of 303 dealers, dealers said they work out of more than 10 systems on average to complete a single transaction, and that those systems don't pass data to each other cleanly (CDK Friction Point Study).

The real cost isn't only the invoice

Dealership software costs show up in more places than the monthly bill:

  • Contracts that outlast the need. A multi-year agreement keeps billing long after the team stops logging in.
  • Tools that do 20% of what you need. Staff still export, copy, paste, and rebuild the report by hand.
  • Training and turnover. Every new hire has to learn every tool, and every departure takes that knowledge out the door.
  • Dependence. When a big vendor goes down, the store goes down with it. In June 2024, a cyberattack on CDK Global knocked out systems used by more than 15,000 retail locations, and many dealers went back to paper forms while it was fixed (Reuters).

What AI changes for dealers

A few years ago, the only way to automate a report or watch an inbox was to buy a product built for that one job. That's no longer true. Today's AI assistants and AI agents, including tools like Grok Bot, can read your reports, CSV exports, and emails. They can pull out what matters, alert the right person, and draft the next step, all from plain-English instructions.

That means much of the work stores rent single-purpose software for can be done in AI tools your people can already access:

  • A GM's morning brief built from the reports you already receive
  • Review alerts for Google, Yelp, and other sites
  • Complaint monitoring in a shared inbox
  • On-demand answers about pace, people, and performance

The catch is the setup. A vague instruction gets a vague result. You're only as good as your prompt.

Why most stores haven't done it yet

It isn't for lack of interest. The usual blockers are:

  • No time. The people who know the work best are the busiest people in the building.
  • No one who knows both sides. IT people rarely know how a desk or a service drive actually runs, and dealer people rarely have time to learn how to write instructions an agent can follow.
  • Fear of making a mess. Nobody wants an AI agent texting customers or touching the DMS without a human in the loop.

These are all solvable. They take dealer experience plus prompt expertise, which is exactly what we do.
