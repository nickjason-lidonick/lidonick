AI Consulting for Car Dealerships: Dealer Experience Plus Prompt Expertise

We know how a dealership runs, and we know how to talk to AI. That combination is rare, and it's the whole job. We sit down with your people, learn where their time goes, and build agents and agentic workflows in your own AI tools to take that work off their plate. When we're done, everything belongs to you.

What an AI consultant for car dealerships actually does

Plenty of companies sell AI to dealers. Lidonick doesn't. Our automotive AI consulting is hands-on setup work, done with your people and inside your accounts:

  • Find the work. We look at the reports, inboxes, follow-ups, and repetitive tasks that eat into each person's day.
  • Pick what AI should handle first. Not everything should be automated. We choose the jobs where an agent saves real time without taking on real risk.
  • Write the prompts. This is the part that makes or breaks an AI agent. We write clear instructions that cover the job, the sources, what counts as important, how the result should look, and what the agent must never do.
  • Set up the agents and workflows. We connect the right sources, set the schedule, and add approval rules so nothing customer-facing goes out without a person's OK.
  • Hand it over. We show your team how it works and how to adjust it, so they don't need us to keep it running.

Why prompt expertise matters

Two stores can use the same AI tool and get completely different results. The difference is almost always the instructions. "Watch my reviews" gets you noise. A good prompt names the sites, sets the bar for what's worth an alert, decides who gets alerted and how, and says the agent must never post a reply on its own.

You're only as good as your prompt. We bring the dealer knowledge to know what matters, and the prompt expertise to make the agent do it the same way every time. For examples, see our Grok Bot prompts for car dealerships.

What dealership AI agents can do

These are examples of the kinds of agents we help stores set up. Every store's setup is different.

For leadership

  • A GM's morning brief covering units, pace against manufacturer objectives and selling days, salesperson performance, and the service drive, built from the reports, CSVs, and forwarded emails you already get
  • On-demand answers about pace, people, and performance

For customer experience

  • Review alerts for Google, Yelp, and other sites, sent to the right person when a very good or very bad review comes in
  • Monitoring a shared inbox for customer complaints so they get flagged before they grow

For the floor, the drive, and the office

  • Follow-up lists and draft texts and emails for salespeople
  • Next-day prep for service
  • Reminders about prep, paperwork, and follow-up for F&I and the BDC
